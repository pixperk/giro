The model

Four ideas, and everything follows

The whole system is a consequence of these. If something below looks surprising, it is one of these being taken seriously.

A posting names both sides

Source, destination, asset, and an amount that is always positive. An entry that does not balance is not something to detect. It cannot be written down.

Volumes, not balances

Each account keeps two counters that only ever increase: everything in, everything out. Balance is the difference. An account that settled millions and now holds nothing is not the same as one never used.

Money is conserved

Value enters from world and leaves to it. Only world may go negative, so every balance summed together is always exactly zero. Watch it below.

The log is the truth

Every change appends an entry whose hash covers the previous one. Editing history invalidates everything after it. The tables are a cache that can be rebuilt from the log.

The unit

A posting, in full

This is the whole vocabulary. Everything the ledger stores is built from records of this shape.

{
  "source":      "users:alice",
  "destination": "users:bob",
  "asset":       "USD/2",
  "amount":      3000
}

Read aloud: $30.00 moved from Alice to Bob. Nothing is missing from that sentence, which is the point. The amount is always positive, because naming both sides already says which way it went.

USD/2 means two decimal places, so 3000 is thirty dollars. The scale lives inside the asset name rather than in a column beside it, so an amount cannot be read without knowing its unit. USD and USD/2 are different assets and never mix.

What that single posting does
AccountInOutBalance
users:alice1000030007000
users:bob300003000

Two rows touched, one counter on each. The same 3000 added twice, so whatever leaves one place arrives somewhere else and the total across all accounts does not move. Do that a million times and it still does not move.

Neither counter ever goes down, including on a reversal. Alice refunded would read 13000 in, 3000 out: back to where she started, with the record that it happened and was undone. A single balance column would forget both.

The boundary

Why there is an account called world

Every posting needs two sides. So write down the first thing that ever happens: Alice deposits $500. What goes in source? Every account is empty. The ledger has just been created.

The money is coming from outside a system that is closed by construction. world is that outside: a bank, a card network, somebody's pocket. The ledger does not know which, only that value crossed its edge.

world is allowed a negative balance, and that is arithmetic rather than privilege. It starts at zero like everything else, so if it could not go negative the first deposit would fail and so would every one after it.

Its balance is a measurement. At −$500 it reads: five hundred dollars entered this ledger and have not left. For a wallet product that number is your total customer liability, and it is the one that should match your bank statement.

Every other account is refused a negative balance unless somebody granted it one, per account and per asset. world carries that permission from creation rather than by being named in the code, so it is the first account to hold it rather than an exception beside the rule.

Think of chips on a poker table. Buying in takes chips from the rack. The rack is not a player and has no stack, and counting it gives you exactly the number of chips in play, negated.

Inside a commit

What happens when you press one of those buttons

One database transaction, in exactly this order. Steps two and three are the whole trick.

  1. Collect every account the postings touchBoth sides of every posting, deduplicated.
  2. Sort them, always the same wayTwo transactions can only deadlock by taking the same locks in opposite orders. A fixed order removes the possibility rather than reducing the odds.
  3. Create any missing rows, then lock them allYou cannot lock a row that does not exist, and asking to lock a missing row silently locks nothing.
  4. Check the resulting balancesReject if any account other than world would end below zero. The check is on the final state, so money may pass through an account within one transaction.
  5. Add the deltasAlways input = input + n, never a value computed earlier. The database does the arithmetic, so a stale read cannot become a stale write.
  6. Take the next id and the chain tipFrom one row, under one lock. Gapless ids and a hash chain need the same lock, so the second is free.
  7. Write the transaction and its per account movesEach move carries the account's balance at that instant, so history is a lookup rather than a replay.
  8. Append the log entry and commitHashed onto the previous entry, in the same transaction as everything above. The log and the data it describes both land or neither does.

Two clocks

When it happened, and when we found out

A settlement file arriving on Tuesday describes Friday. A card capture settles days after authorisation. Anything touching the outside world hears late, so every transaction carries both dates and they are allowed to disagree.

1 MarchAlice is funded+100
3 MarchAlice is paid again+50
5 MarchAlice pays out−30
2 Marcha settlement file arrives on the 8th, describing the 2nd+50

Ask what Alice held on 3 March and the answer is 200, not 150. The backdated payment belongs in second place, and everything after it shifts.

So each movement stores the running balance twice: once frozen in the order the ledger learned things, and once in the order they actually happened. The first answers what did we believe at the time, which is what an audit asks. The second answers what was true on that date, which is what a report asks.

Only the second is ever rewritten. Keeping both is what separates a reconciliation discrepancy from a bug: it lets you say the system was correct and the information arrived late.

Scope

What it deliberately does not decide

The ledger records movements. It does not judge them. Everything below is somebody else's decision, and keeping them out is what lets this stay small enough to trust.

Who may move money

Authentication and authorisation are upstream. This service trusts its caller, which is why it belongs behind your API rather than in front of your users.

Whether a payment is allowed

Fraud rules, sanctions, limits, holds. All policy, all decided before a posting is written.

What an exchange rate should be

Converting $100 to €92 is two postings committed together. The ledger records that both happened and has no opinion on the rate.

Whether real money moved

A deposit says value crossed this ledger's edge. Confirming cash reached a bank account is reconciliation, against a statement.

What an account means

users:alice:wallet is a string. The hierarchy is your convention; the ledger indexes it without understanding it.

How to retry

It makes retrying safe. Send the same Idempotency-Key and the original transaction comes back rather than a second one. Send that key with different contents and it is an error, not a silent success for a payment that never happened.
